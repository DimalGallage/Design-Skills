# 5 · Organic & Human

> **Main job:** build a warm relationship, so people feel cared for and share your values.
> **Feels:** warm, approachable, honest, a little imperfect.
> **Expression level:** 2–4 of 5.

## 1. Essence

This is the web's answer to "clinical" design and to machine-perfect, AI-generated sameness. 2026 trend reports agree that people want **warmth, texture and emotional connection**: hand-drawn elements, expressive type, organic shapes and deliberate imperfection. Under names such as **neo-naturalism** and **biophilic design**, the category draws on nature: earthy palettes (forest green, terracotta, clay, sand), soft edges, flowing shapes, asymmetric layouts instead of hard grids, paper and grain textures, and real faces.

Trust here comes from **warmth rather than authority**.

## 2. How it differs from the other categories

| vs. | Difference |
|---|---|
| Institutional Trust | Institutional says "we are competent"; Organic says "we care". Grids and blue vs. curves and clay. |
| Quiet Luxury | Both use calm palettes and space, but Luxury is distant and perfect; Organic is close and imperfect: people smile at the camera, and textures are touchable. |
| Bold Expressive | Both have personality, but Bold is loud and ironic; Organic is soft and sincere. Bold uses clashing neon; Organic uses harmonious earth tones. |
| Product-Led Tech | Tech is cool and precise. Organic tech products (wellness apps, fertility trackers) intentionally escape that feeling. |
| Editorial | Organic often borrows editorial type (soft serifs), but its layouts are rounder and more illustrative. |

## 3. Where to use it

**Best for:** wellness, yoga, meditation, mental health · food and beverage, restaurants, cafés, bakeries, farms · sustainable/eco brands, outdoor gear, zero-waste · organic beauty and personal care · nonprofits, charities, community groups · childcare, schools, family services · consumer health (clinics, therapy, fertility, pharmacy; blend with 2 for booking and compliance) · pet brands · local businesses and crafts · plant, garden and home brands · travel and eco-tourism.

**Use cases:** brand homepages, D2C shops, recipe and menu sites, donation pages, booking sites for practitioners, app marketing sites for wellness apps.

**Avoid or blend** for enterprise buyers, regulated finance, or anywhere the audience equates softness with lack of rigor.

## 4. Signature traits

- **Earthy, desaturated palette:** sage, olive, moss, terracotta, clay, ochre, sand, cream, warm charcoal.
- **Organic shapes:** blobs, arches, pebble-like cards, wavy section dividers, large radii.
- **Hand-made marks:** hand-drawn illustrations, doodled underlines and arrows, scribble circles, handwritten accents.
- **Texture:** paper grain, risograph noise, subtle shadows that look photographed, natural materials in photos.
- **Soft serif or rounded sans type:** friendly, with some quirk.
- **Real people and places:** founders, farmers, staff, customers; natural light; unpolished moments.
- **Values made visible:** certifications (B Corp, organic, Fairtrade), sourcing maps, impact numbers, transparency reports.
- **Gentle, springy motion:** things bloom, sway, wobble slightly.

## 5. Design tokens

### Typography
- **Display:** soft or quirky serifs (Fraunces with the "wonk"/soft axes, Recoleta, Cooper, GT Alpina, Gambarino, Roca, Young Serif, DM Serif), or rounded/humanist sans (Nunito, Quicksand, Poppins, Outfit, Circular, Manrope).
- **Accent:** a handwritten or brush face (Caveat, Reenie Beanie) **only for 1–3 words**, or better, lettering drawn by hand as SVG.
- **Body:** a humanist sans (Inter, DM Sans, Work Sans) or book serif at 17–19px.
- **Scale:** 1.25–1.333. Use sentence case; avoid shouting caps.

### Color
- Backgrounds: cream `#F6F1E7`, oat `#EDE6D8`, soft sage `#E4EADF`.
- Ink: warm charcoal `#2B2622` (not pure black).
- Accents: terracotta `#C0633F`, moss `#4F6B3A`, ochre `#C99A2E`, dusty rose `#D8A7A0`.
- Sections alternate between tinted backgrounds for rhythm.
- **Watch contrast.** Earthy mid-tones on cream often fail 4.5:1. Use the darkest shade of each hue for text and buttons.

### Spacing and shape
- 8px base; airy (section spacing 80–128px).
- **Radius:** 16–32px on cards, pill buttons, arch-topped images (`border-radius: 999px 999px 0 0`), SVG blob masks.
- Wavy or hand-drawn SVG dividers between sections.

### Depth
- Soft, warm, diffuse shadows (`0 10px 30px rgba(80,60,40,.10)`), or none. Paper/cut-out layering.

### Motion
- Springy, organic easing (`cubic-bezier(.34,1.56,.64,1)` for small overshoots). Line drawings that animate their strokes. Leaves or shapes that drift slightly. Hover wiggle on stickers.
- Keep it gentle and optional (`prefers-reduced-motion`).

### Imagery
- Natural light, warm grade, visible texture (wood, linen, soil, skin). Candid, diverse people.
- Illustration in a consistent hand-made style (gouache, crayon, line) commissioned for the brand.
- Food: overhead and hands-in-frame shots, with process not just the finished plate.

## 6. Page anatomy

**D2C / brand homepage:**
1. Friendly header: logo (often lettering), 4–5 nav items, cart. Maybe a warm announcement bar ("Free shipping over $50 🌿").
2. **Hero:** warm lifestyle photo or illustration, a human-sounding promise ("Skincare that's kind to you and the planet"), one CTA.
3. Values strip: 3–4 icons (hand-drawn) + short claims, backed by certifications.
4. Bestsellers in soft rounded cards with star ratings.
5. **Founder/origin story:** photo of real people + a short narrative.
6. Ingredients/sourcing: map or illustrated diagram.
7. Reviews/UGC: customer photos, quotes with names.
8. Impact numbers: trees planted, plastic avoided, donations.
9. Journal/recipes (blends with Editorial).
10. Newsletter with an incentive, in a friendly voice.
11. Warm footer: hand-drawn flourish, contact, social links, certifications.

**Restaurant / café:** hero photo → hours and location *immediately* → menu (HTML, not PDF) → reservations/order online → story → gallery → events → map.

**Nonprofit:** mission hero with a real beneficiary photo → impact numbers → "How your money helps" tiers → stories → prominent **Donate** (with recurring option) → transparency (financial reports) → volunteer.

**Wellness practitioner / clinic:** calming hero → services → practitioner profiles with photos and credentials → **online booking** → pricing/insurance → testimonials → FAQs. Borrow Institutional forms and accessibility.

## 7. Key components

Blob/arch image masks · hand-drawn underline/circle highlights · sticker badges · wavy dividers · soft rounded product cards · review cards with photos · recipe card · ingredient spotlight · impact counter · donation amount selector · booking widget · map with illustrated pins.

## 8. Copy and voice

Warm, first person plural ("we"), conversational, honest about limits ("We're not perfect, here's what we're working on"). Sensory for food and wellness. Keep claims backed up; greenwashing destroys this category's trust.

## 9. Pitfalls

- **Twee overload:** too many doodles, fonts and blobs makes things childish and cluttered. Pick one hand-made device and repeat it.
- **Contrast failures** with sage/clay text on cream.
- **Script fonts in body text or buttons:** hard to read and to translate.
- **Heavy textures** (large PNG grain overlays) hurting performance. Use tiny tiling images or SVG noise filters.
- **PDF menus** and hidden opening hours on restaurant sites.
- **Stock photos** of "diverse smiling people" undermine authenticity.

## 10. Sub-styles

| Sub-style | Traits | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Neo-naturalism / biophilic | Earth palette, natural textures, flowing layouts | Eco brands, outdoor, wellness |
| Hand-drawn / craft | Illustration-led, doodles, lettering | Food, kids, local business, creators |
| Soft retro / 70s warm | Rounded serifs (Cooper/Recoleta), mustard/rust, sunburst shapes | Coffee, beverages, lifestyle |
| Calm digital | Pastels, rounded sans, blob gradients, gentle illustration | Meditation/mental-health apps |
| Farm-to-table | Documentary photography, kraft textures, serif | Restaurants, producers |

## 12. Launch checklist

- [ ] Real people and real places on key pages; no generic stock
- [ ] Earth-tone text and buttons checked for 4.5:1 / 3:1
- [ ] One consistent hand-made device, not five
- [ ] Values backed by certifications, numbers or reports
- [ ] Essential info (hours, location, prices, booking, donate) visible without scrolling far
- [ ] Textures and illustrations optimized (SVG, AVIF, small tiles)
- [ ] Motion is gentle and turns off with reduced-motion preference
